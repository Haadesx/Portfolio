title: "ICONOCLAST: Evaluating Targeted LLM Representation Edits"
---

## Motivation

Directional abliteration can reduce unwanted refusals in an open-weight language model, but the edit often costs utility: benign requests may be refused, or the next-token distribution may drift from the base model.

ICONOCLAST asks whether a more careful edit can change the target behavior with less collateral damage, and whether that tradeoff can be measured instead of asserted.

## Method

The framework has four stages:

1. Collect residual activations for harmful and benign prompts during one-token generation.
2. Estimate per-layer candidate directions using mean, median, variance-scaled, and hybrid contrastive estimators.
3. Estimate a low-rank benign PCA subspace from harmless residuals and project the candidate directions out of that subspace.
4. Apply rank-one or low-rank LoRA edits to attention-output and MLP down-projection modules, then search layer, component, strength, and direction settings with Optuna.

Evaluation tracks harmful-prompt refusals (20 JailbreakBench holdout prompts), benign overrefusals (64 Alpaca holdout prompts), disclaimer markers, heuristic task compliance, and first-token KL divergence from the base model on benign prompts.

## What the numbers actually show

Ten matched open-weight model comparisons are reported. The repository's selection rule sorts by fewer harmful refusals, then fewer benign overrefusals, then lower KL. Under that rule, ICONOCLAST wins all ten rows. It has strictly fewer harmful refusals in six rows and lower KL in eight of ten.

The tradeoffs matter as much as the wins:

| Model | ICONOCLAST refusals / overrefusals / KL | HERETIC refusals / overrefusals / KL | Read |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct | 0/20 · 0/64 · 0.0447 | 1/20 · 0/64 · 0.1854 | ICONOCLAST behavior and utility win |
| Falcon3-7B-Instruct | 0/20 · 0/64 · 6.1448 | 4/20 · 1/64 · 0.1648 | Behavior win, but a severe KL outlier |
| Yi-1.5-9B-Chat | 2/20 · 0/64 · 0.0511 | 3/20 · 0/64 · 0.0355 | Fewer refusals, but HERETIC has lower KL |

This is an optimized holdout benchmark, not broad statistical confirmation. A deployment system should impose a hard KL ceiling; the Falcon3 row shows why a "refusals removed" headline on its own is not enough.

## Tooling and artifacts

- Python package and CLI for the full abliteration/study workflow.
- Contrastive activation analysis and per-layer direction estimation.
- Benign-subspace projection before editing.
- Optuna-based search and repeatable evaluation.
- Unit tests.
- Six released edited model artifacts described by the project.

## My contribution

It is the standalone public artifact split from the broader `NLP_Project` research workspace.

## Limits

- No large-N evaluator outputs were available; the reported numbers are holdout-optimized experiments.
- The framework changes refusal behavior; it does not provide a safety guarantee, nor does it prove that a model is universally "uncensored" or unchanged.
- KL divergence is a utility proxy, not a complete measure of capability.
- The evidence supports a research framework and a set of tradeoffs, not a production compliance claim.
