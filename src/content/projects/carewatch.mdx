## The problem

Caregivers can't watch hours of footage. CareWatch tests whether computer vision can surface the few interactions that deserve **human review**, with enough context to work out what happened.

This is a safety-review prototype, not a validated abuse detector. The point is evidence and escalation, not an automated judgment.

## Why pose-based heuristics

A full action-recognition model needs labeled examples a weekend build doesn't have. Pose estimation gives a more inspectable first pass:

- Track people and body keypoints with YOLOv8-pose.
- Flag proximity and fast-motion interactions, or long caretaker absence.
- Send only flagged frames to a vision model for scene verification.
- Capture a short evidence clip around the event.

The tradeoff is deliberate. Heuristics are explainable and cheap, but they will produce false positives and can miss subtle events.

## System flow

```text
camera frames
  → YOLOv8 pose + person tracking
  → proximity / motion heuristics
  → scene verification (hosted or local vision model)
  → 30-second rolling evidence buffer
  → push alert + SQLite event log
  → React dashboard / iOS guardian client
```

The repository splits responsibilities across `core/detector.py`, `core/classifier.py`, `core/clip_saver.py`, `core/alert.py`, and `core/pipeline.py`. Alerts are deduplicated by severity. The dashboard handles live monitoring and review of saved recordings; the iOS client is the guardian-facing surface.

## Evaluation and recognition

CareWatch won **Best Social Good Hack at Bitcamp 2026**, verified on [Devpost](https://devpost.com/software/eyeq-0c8wdl).

The award recognizes the prototype and how it frames the problem. It is not a precision/recall result. The repository does not publish sensitivity, false-positive rates, or a clinical validation study. The demo runs staged high-risk interactions and caretaker-absence scenarios.

## Team and contribution

CareWatch was a team hackathon build. I worked on the pose-heuristic pipeline, evidence capture, and review workflow; teammates contributed to the surrounding application and iOS client. The repository is the source of truth for the combined implementation and has no complete file-by-file authorship ledger, so this page does not attribute every module to me.

## Limits

- No claim of real-world abuse-detection reliability.
- No clinical, legal, or safeguarding validation.
- False positives are expected. Camera angle, occlusion, lighting, and unusual motion all matter.
- A 30-second clip is evidence for a human, not a complete account of an event.
- The project should not be deployed as a decision-making system without consent, review, and a much stronger evaluation program.
