## Context
During my internship at **Arcascope**, I tackled the challenge of quantifying and predicting daily fatigue using data from wearable devices. Fatigue is subjective and noisy, making standard regression approaches prone to overfitting.

## Methodology
We approached this as an **ordinal regression** problem rather than simple regression or classification.

### Key Innovations:
1. **CORAL (Consistent Rank Logits) Layer:**
   We replaced the standard output layer with a CORAL layer to enforce the ordinal nature of fatigue scores (e.g., Scale 1-10).
   
2. **Loss Function Engineering:**
   Combined `CornLoss` (Conditional Ordinal Regression) with standard MSE to penalize large deviations while respecting rank order.

3. **Physics-Informed Features:**
   Integrated **Runge-Kutta** methods to model circadian rhythm dynamics directly into the feature set.

## Results vs Baseline
We iterated through ~20 architectures. The final model significantly outperformed the baseline:

| Metric | Baseline (Linear Reg) | Deep Ordinal Model | Improvement |
|:-------|:---------------------|:-------------------|:------------|
| MAE    | 1.17                 | **0.25**           | **78%**     |
| Accuracy| 52% (within ±1)     | **85%**            | +33%        |

This reduction in Mean Absolute Error (MAE) allowed the system to provide actionable daily alerts to users.

> **Evidence note.** The MAE and accuracy figures are self-reported from the original project; no public dataset, repository, or evaluation artifact is linked, and the figures have not been independently reproduced in this portfolio. "Accuracy" is measured as predictions within ±1 of the reported fatigue score; the validation protocol and participant-level split are not documented here.
